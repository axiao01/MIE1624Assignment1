  • Remote developers are paid more than hybrid developers. After removing implausible salary entries, the average remote salary is $115,890 versus $95,155 for hybrid. The gap is far too large to be due to chance, and every method we used agrees.
  • The gap holds for the typical employee too. Median pay is $98,022 (remote) versus $81,210 (hybrid).
  • In the US and Canada, a graduate degree is worth about $24.2k a year. Master's and professional-degree holders earn similar amounts, and both earn clearly more than Bachelor's holders.
  • What this means for you: offering remote roles is associated with a higher-paid talent pool, so budget accordingly when benchmarking remote offers. Requiring a Master's versus a professional degree makes no pay difference, but either one versus a Bachelor's does. These are associations from survey data, not proof that job mode or education causes the pay difference (location, seniority and role also play a part).

1. Data overview

Before comparing groups we looked at satisfaction and pay across job titles and ages to spot data problems.

What this means: most roles report similar satisfaction (about 7 out of 10). Executives and founders are the happiest, while QA and support roles are the least satisfied. One response of "60" on a 0–10 scale was a data error and was removed, and the "Retired" score of 10 comes from a single person. On pay, "Financial analyst" looks like the best-paid role only because one person reported $18.4M; its median is just $87k. Averages can be badly distorted by a few extreme answers.
What this means: pay rises quickly through the 20s and 30s and levels off at about $100k from the early 40s. We also found impossible ages (1, 3, 9, 126, 151) and 269 salaries below $1,000 a year, most likely monthly pay typed as yearly. Entries like these must be cleaned before comparing groups.

What this means: the p-value answers the question "if remote and hybrid developers were really paid the same on average, how likely is a gap of $20,734 or more?" The answer is about 2 in 1059, which is effectively impossible. The gap is real, not a sampling fluke.

Methodology & caveats

  • Data: the cleaned 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey (20,407 respondents with a reported salary, converted to USD).
  • Outliers: removed within each group using 1.5 × IQR fences on log salary (Hybrid 615, Remote 553 removed; education groups 4.6–5.6%).
  • Bootstrap: 10,000 replications, each group resampled with replacement at its own size, fixed random seed (1624).
  • All numbers on this page are generated by the same code as the submitted analysis notebook. Only aggregated results are published.
  • Survey data are self-reported and observational: differences are associations and may reflect country, experience or job role.
